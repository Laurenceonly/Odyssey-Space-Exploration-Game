# Odyssey — Design direction and brainstorming record

Updated: 2026-10-14 (Asia/Manila).

This records the conversation, not an implementation specification. No gameplay changes were made as part of brainstorming. Agreed direction does not imply every feature must ship immediately. Prices, hosting estimates, names, and scope proposals are provisional.

## Purpose and identity

Odyssey began as a game for the creator’s brother to explore space and learn. The intended experience is welcoming, enjoyable, and suitable for families, without manipulative spending or harmful social interactions. Parent approval is an aspiration to test with families, not a guarantee.

Keep Odyssey as the title for now. It fits the journey, although related games already use the word. Naming searches are not proof of exclusive availability. Numerous subtitles and coined alternatives were brainstormed, but none was selected. No player/community name is finalized; candidates included Voyagers, Starfarers, Trailblazers, Pioneers, Navigators, Starseekers, and Odysseans.

The distinctive direction is an approachable solar-system adventure combining learning, discovery, personal memories, small camps, and exploration with friends. Do not claim the concept or title is globally unique.

## Solo, multiplayer, and progression — agreed direction

- Solo means exploring on your own; it does not mean everything is unlocked.
- Multiplayer lets friends explore together, with the same core activities and learning content.
- Remove the fully unlocked Exploration mode as the main play option in the future design. Existing code has not been removed.
- Start near Earth with a short introduction to flying and scanning.
- Make multiplayer available after the introduction; unlocking every planet is not a prerequisite.
- Unlock destinations through exploration in either mode, retaining personal progress across both.
- Friends with different progress can travel together and help one another; each completes their own discoveries.

Still to decide: exactly what unlocking grants, whether unknown worlds remain freely reachable, and how group discoveries are credited. Suggested unlocks included names, Field Guide facts, navigation routes, and landing sites.

Independent multiplayer travel was discussed: one person could explore Mars while another visits Earth, with optional meetups. This is a proposed direction, not a settled server/world architecture. Private room codes, invitations, and an initial two-player prototype were recommended; final capacity is undecided.

## Planets, landing, and learning

Make every planetary destination explorable, using believable fictional technology when necessary. Giant planets have no solid walking surface; fictional stations, atmospheric platforms, probes, or nearby moons can support visits. Clearly distinguish real science from invented technology in the Field Guide.

Different destinations should have different places, activities, gravity experiences, and environmental conditions. The creator expressed interest in situational weather. Weather should affect decisions and offer activities rather than simply forcing players to wait. The Moon has no ordinary atmospheric weather; sunlight, shadows, and temperature can vary its experience instead.

Contained landing zones were recommended as an achievable approach: a crater, valley, lake, or station rather than an entire continuous planetary surface. Multiple zones and return visits are possibilities; their number and scope remain undecided.

Example learning activities included jumping in different gravity, investigating craters and rocks, comparing shadows, measuring conditions, and following evidence of ancient water. These are ideas for later development, not commitments to implement now.

## Ship interiors and scale — latest explicit priorities

- Add a walkable ship interior: the creator approved exploring your own ship.
- Watching planets through ship windows is a particularly important, explicitly welcomed idea.
- Preserve the existing first-person viewpoint and third-person on-screen ship size, position, angle, and field of view when adding interiors or changing scale. The creator explicitly reaffirmed desktop fit and the same POV as before; improve planetary scale without shrinking the ship on screen.
- Improve planet-to-ship scale: the spaceship currently feels too large during exploration; planets should feel vast and more realistic.
- Exact radii, proportions, orbital distances, ship dimensions, and travel times remain undecided. Do not assume full astronomical scale is required.

A compact interior usable while landed or safely parked was recommended as a first step. Cockpit, equipment area, personal corner, and airlock were suggested. Friends boarding and walking inside a moving ship are later possibilities and substantially more complex.

## Backpacks, gathering, building, and camps

The creator prefers collecting materials and building from them over only placing ready-made kits. Backpacks would carry materials, tools, and supplies. The proposed rhythm is explore, gather, build, and pack or recover equipment.

Shelters/houses could provide an expedition home, weather protection, and places to display discoveries. Avoid cluttering planets or allowing unrestricted building everywhere; this concern was explicitly raised.

Suggested safeguards, not finalized rules:

- Designated buildable camp areas and protected landmarks/waterways.
- Limits on shelters and equipment; one active camp or shelter per player was suggested.
- Relocating or packing structures rather than leaving abandoned clutter.
- Recover materials when dismantling structures.
- Owner permission before visitors alter a camp.
- Only showing relevant private-session camps rather than all players’ buildings.

Resource types, gathering rules, recipes, inventory capacity, persistence, and whether construction uses prefabs or separate pieces remain open.

## Vehicles and companions — brainstormed, not finalized

Boats could explore Earth lakes and islands; rovers could suit other landing zones. Start with one vehicle and a meaningful destination rather than many vehicles without activities.

Pets/companions were discussed, including a robot companion as an initial space-friendly option. Following, waiting, reacting, or pointing out observations were suggested. Avoid punishment or hunger obligations when a player is away. Animal companions and additional companion types remain possibilities.

## Profiles, friends, and communication

The creator wants personal profiles and the ability to add friends. Suggested profile content: nickname, astronaut appearance, earned mission patches, discoveries, photos, and friend list. Do not require real names or public personal details.

Use a small curated set of emoji reactions; the creator approved limited emojis rather than unrestricted messaging. Examples included hello, ready, launch, explore, look here, photo time, help, and celebration. Labels, location pings, cooldowns, muting, leaving, and blocking were suggested.

Mutual friend requests and private invitations are recommended. Parent-controlled social permissions were discussed. Text chat, direct messages, voice, public search, matchmaking, and moderation scope remain undecided; private play with preset communication is the recommended initial approach. Friendly emojis do not eliminate harassment risk.

## Free play and premium support — agreed direction

Core exploration and multiplayer should be free. Players start with a complete, attractive astronaut suit. Optional paid outfits offer personalization, not abilities or access.

Later premium possibilities discussed:

- Ship paint and cosmetic exterior styles.
- Cabin decorations and appearance packs.
- Shelter appearance variations with equivalent function.
- Companion appearance variations if companions are introduced.

Start with outfits only was the recommendation. Free customization and earned patches/decorations should remain worthwhile. Tools, backpack capacity, protection, performance, and destination access should be earned through play rather than premium purchases.

Avoid random purchase boxes, virtual currency that hides prices, countdown offers, and repeated purchase prompts. Show exact items, previews, and real-money prices; keep the shop outside expeditions.

Prices such as $2 per suit or $5 per collection were illustrative, not decided. Cosmetic sales depend on an audience and may not cover hosting. Earlier paid-full-game and expedition-pack models were explored, but free core play with optional cosmetics is the latest chosen direction.

## Creator page, contributions, and feedback — agreed concept

Include a creator page identifying the creator and explaining that Odyssey began for their brother. Offer optional contributions/support, contact or feedback, credits, and development updates.

Support does not grant gameplay advantages. A dedicated project email or feedback form was recommended instead of personal contact details. Younger players can ask a parent to contact the creator; do not solicit children’s personal information. Contribution platform, payment flow, contact address, and page wording are undecided.

## Reasons to return

Aim for voluntary return through curiosity and enjoyable experiences, not pressure to play forever. Ideas included distinctive locations, meaningful environmental changes, connected clues, earnable tools, personal camps, photos, journal/atlas progress, and adventures with friends.

Repeated random tasks alone are unlikely to produce interesting variety. Combine activities and conditions meaningfully. A satisfying finite main journey with optional exploration and later additions is acceptable.

## Inspiration and deferred ideas

Official examples reviewed:

- Astroneer: https://astroneer.space/ — exploration and collaborative bases/vehicles.
- No Man’s Sky: https://www.nomanssky.com/worlds-part-ii-update/ — environmental variety, weather, and hazards.
- Minecraft: https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/minecraft-tips-for-beginners — player-chosen goals and creation.
- Outer Wilds: https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/outer-wilds.html — connected mysteries and changing locations.

Borrow manageable principles rather than attempting all their systems. These comparisons are design interpretations, not evidence of guaranteed retention or success.

Deferred following the creator’s latest steering: shipboard science lab, spacewalks, Earth observatory, additional research/repair expedition systems, cooperative instrument projects, expanded atlas, and other newly suggested activities. Window views and perceived scale are the latest priorities.

## Budget and practical scope

The creator wants a low-cost approach. Share and test the current single-player game before committing to paid infrastructure. Free hosting can support early experiments within provider limits; a small continuously available multiplayer server was roughly budgeted at $5–$15/month, dependent on actual usage. These are conversation-time estimates, not guaranteed costs or capacity. Verify current prices and commercial-use terms before deployment or monetization.

Multiplayer requires session management, a shared simulation clock/state, smooth networked movement, disconnect handling, hosting, and testing. Current saves and simulation are browser-local. A large coordinating main.js also suggests separating simulation, rendering, and UI before networking.

Recommended staging, not a committed schedule:

1. Test with a small group and improve the first experience.
2. Build one complete solo landing expedition.
3. Grow toward Earth, Moon, and Mars with contained zones.
4. Test two-player private co-op before broader multiplayer.
5. Expand destinations and additional systems gradually.

No deadline, final feature order, hosting provider, payment platform, or player capacity is settled. Development time estimates were rough; revenue examples were arithmetic, not forecasts.

## October 15 direction — Distinct worlds to land on

The creator wants every planet to be explorable, with its own feature and characteristics. Fiction should draw on real planetary identity rather than literal surface feasibility. Giant planets therefore have imagined walkable landscapes: Jupiter’s storm terraces, Saturn’s ringlit ice fields, Uranus’s tilted frost canyon, and Neptune’s wind-carved expanse. Stations and atmospheric landing platforms do not fulfill this experience. All worlds need different landforms and landmarks, rather than a recolored shared area.

Real science stays in the Field Guide and discovery notes. Imagined giant-planet terrain, sky arrangements and playable gravity are explicitly fictional; actual gas/ice giants have no walkable ground. Rocky worlds retain representative geology, atmosphere and measured gravity. Keep the existing Earth, flight views and saves.

The flight key guide and its Settings visibility toggle are removed at the creator’s request. Keyboard flight controls remain available.
