# Planet visits

Agreed direction, October 15, 2026: land and explore every planet in its own way. Build distinct fictional landscapes inspired by real planetary characteristics. Giant-planet visits use imagined terrain, rather than orbital stations or atmospheric landing platforms. One bounded zone per world is the first step; these are not entire continuous planets.

| World | Terrain and landmark | Science guiding the fiction |
|---|---|---|
| Mercury | Rugged impact rim with exposed layers and a larger-looking Sun | Craters, thin exosphere, 3.70 m/s² gravity |
| Venus | Hazy volcanic plain, clusters of hexagonal basalt | Volcanic geology, dense CO₂, extreme heat and pressure, 8.87 m/s² gravity |
| Earth | Existing wooded lake shore, fallen branch | Surface liquid water, life, 9.81 m/s² gravity |
| Moon | Gray basin with secondary craters and a reflector | Regolith, impacts, Earth view, 1.62 m/s² gravity |
| Mars | Dry channel, layered outcrop, rounded stones and dust | Ancient-water evidence, oxidized iron, thin CO₂, 3.71 m/s² gravity |
| Jupiter | Amber cloudstone terraces, large rotating cyclone | Banded atmosphere and long-lived storms |
| Saturn | Pale dunes, a crystalline grove, rings across the sky | Ring particles are predominantly ice, with rock and dust |
| Uranus | Cyan canyon with leaning ice walls | Blue-green appearance and about 98° axial tilt |
| Neptune | Blue wind-carved ridges and an arch, swift streamers | Methane, strong winds and a changing atmosphere |

The four giant planets have no solid surface for a walk. Their zones are fantasy interpretations: cloudstone, ice fields, terrain, ring views, local wind animation and tuned gravity are invented. The visible game copy explains that distinction. Ice giant does not mean a walkable frozen crust. Venus’s suit/ship protection is imaginary; real spacesuits cannot support a surface walk there. All local terrain, equipment and landforms are representative reconstructions rather than surveyed landing coordinates or live weather measurements.

Every map currently has a circular playable radius of **104 metres**: **208 metres across**, approximately **33,979 m² (3.4 hectares / 0.034 km²)**. The rendered ground plane is 440 × 440 metres, with scenery beyond the playable boundary. These are local exploration zones, not full planets. Open-ground crossing takes about 74 seconds walking or 37 seconds running before hills and obstacles.

Earth supports shallow-water wading and buoyant deep-water swimming, including a stronger Shift stroke and Space/J water kick. Reachable rocks, basalt columns, ice formations and local landmarks use their transformed mesh surfaces for standing, side collisions and overhead clearance. Jump height depends on the local gravity; tall formations cannot all be climbed in one jump.

Planet previews fit a common visual envelope inside the space left by the panels and HUD, with extra room for rings. This is presentation framing rather than a true-size comparison.

Each visit supports arrival, disembarking, walking, running, jumping, camera changes, a named landmark interaction, a real scientific observation, photos, boarding and departure. Landscape shapes, geometry, haze, lighting, motion and gravity distinguish the worlds; consistent controls keep them learnable. Orbital cameras and existing game-mode saves remain supported. Refresh in the same tab restores the visited world, surface position (including rocks and water), camera choice and viewing direction. Cabin position/look also resumes. These positions use session storage; a fresh browser session starts normally, while discoveries/photos/logs keep their existing persistent saves per game mode. Larger 2.4 × 1.35m survey flags on 5m poles mark the landmarks. The persistent Find ship control shows direction and horizontal distance; clicking turns the camera toward the ship without teleporting the astronaut. An on-screen marker labels the ship when it fits outside the guidance/HUD areas. Earth’s fallen log and twig follow the terrain at both ends.
